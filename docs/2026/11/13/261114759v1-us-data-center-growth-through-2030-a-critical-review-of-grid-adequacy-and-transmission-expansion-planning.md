## TLDR
This paper critically reviews U.S. data center growth and grid/transmission outlook through 2030, arguing that transmission expansion planning must incorporate validated data-center fault-response and demand-recovery dynamics—not just aggregate demand forecasts—to determine feasible connection capacity, required reinforcements, and preferred transmission investments.

## Abstract
The growth of large data centers raises questions about both supply adequacy and the suitability of load representations used in connection studies. This paper critically reviews the U.S. demand, generation, and transmission outlook through 2030 and examines the implications for transmission expansion planning (TEP). Comparison of public forecasts highlights distinctions among annual average demand, peak demand, and grid connection capacity. National generating-capacity totals cannot establish a dependable surplus because resource availability, regional adequacy, transmission constraints, and project timing determine supply capability. Recent grid events and published dynamic studies demonstrate that faults can trigger substantial data-center transfers to backup supply, followed by delayed or coordinated demand recovery. These behaviors require appropriate representation within the conventional planning framework, alongside assessment of the requested maximum grid import. The selected literature reveals limited integration of validated data-center dynamic models into the technical screening and economic comparison of expansion alternatives. The review identifies the need to establish whether fault response and recovery change feasible connection capacity, required reinforcement, or the preferred transmission investment.
