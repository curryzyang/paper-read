## Abstract
Power systems resilience planning requires evaluating many hazard scenarios and investment portfolios, often requiring network reduction for computational tractability. The resulting aggregation, however, can obscure community impact data needed for equitable planning. We present a network reduction method that jointly preserves network physics and the spatial distribution of community data. We embed a household-level scarce resource score that characterizes outage impact within optimization-based Kron reductions with two formulations: a scarcity-weighted method that prioritizes vulnerable nodes, and a scarcity-constrained method that bounds data dissimilarity within aggregated clusters. Both are evaluated on a realistic distribution feeder of Galveston Island, Texas and benchmarked against multiple approaches. The scarcity-constrained method performs best, achieving 86% reduction with worst-case voltage errors below 0.01p.u. while preserving the spatial distribution of community data. By explicitly controlling data heterogeneity within and across clusters, the proposed method yields reduced networks that retain decision-relevant community information without sacrificing electrical accuracy.
