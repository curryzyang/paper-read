## Abstract
We present an analogue of the classical Gittins index for a one-armed decision problem with a continuous spectrum of payoffs. The model assumes that the decision-maker observes independent realizations of a random variable and, at each step, decides whether to accept the current opportunity or continue observing. We show that the optimal strategy takes the form of a threshold rule, while the corresponding reservation index is determined by a one-dimensional fixed-point equation with a direct decision-theoretic interpretation. The model is illustrated with an example of bookmaker betting related to horse racing and the Kelly criterion. This perspective allows the proposed index to be viewed as a threshold of informational advantage. This, in turn, points to potential applications in optimal stopping problems and decision-making under uncertainty.
