## TLDR
The paper proves that Dini-derivative and Clarke-subdifferential inequalities with constant \(\gamma\) characterize strong quasiconvexity with the exact same modulus \(\gamma\), rather than only \(\gamma/2\), resolving several open questions and yielding sharp optimization consequences.

## Abstract
In this paper, we study first-order characterizations of strong quasiconvexity for functions defined on convex subsets of real normed spaces. For functions that are continuous along segments, an inequality involving the lower Dini derivative is a known necessary condition for strong quasiconvexity with a given modulus, whereas the established converse guarantees only half of that modulus. By means of a one-dimensional lemma, we prove that this inequality, and even its weaker version with the upper Dini derivative, ensures strong quasiconvexity with the same modulus, without requiring the domain to be open; this answers a question raised in the recent literature. The main result leads to a characterization with the same modulus in terms of the Clarke subdifferential for locally Lipschitz functions on Banach spaces, which answers another open question, and to a formula for the exact modulus of radial functions defined on Hilbert spaces. We also present some consequences in optimization, such as an optimality condition without Clarke regularity, an error bound with optimal constant and, for the projected subgradient method, the same convergence rate as in the strongly convex case, together with the exponential convergence of the associated flow. Finally, we show that the converse of a relation between proximal points and strong subdifferentials fails in general, but holds for a truncation of the function.
