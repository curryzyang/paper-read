## TLDR
Frequency-scan impedance assessments of one proprietary and three public data-center models show that only UPSs in double-conversion mode present negative resistance across the sub-synchronous band—making lightly loaded data centers the worst SSCI case—while bypass-mode UPSs and other facility branches remain passive, providing a basis for screening data centers during interconnection studies.

## Abstract
Data centers are now one of the fastest-growing loads on the bulk power system, and most of their demand reaches the grid through power converters inside uninterruptible power supplies (UPS). Whether these loads can take part in sub-synchronous control interaction (SSCI), as wind and HVDC converters do, has not been studied extensively. We examine this question with impedance-based frequency scans at the point of common coupling (PCC). Using a detailed proprietary model (INS-ISU), we scan the motor, variable-frequency-drive (VFD), lighting, and UPS-fed information-technology (IT) branches of a representative facility separately. Only the UPS in double-conversion mode shows negative resistance; it covers the whole sub-synchronous band and continues up to about 170 Hz. The other branches, and the UPS in bypass mode, stay passive. We then repeat the scans on three public models from PNNL, TAMU, and NLR. None of them shows negative resistance in bypass mode, while three of the four models do in double-conversion mode. Lowering the load from rated to light load makes the negative resistance deeper, as expected for a constant-power load, so a lightly loaded facility in double-conversion mode is the worst case. Raising the UPS DC-link capacitance pushes the negative resistance into a narrow band around the fundamental. These findings give a basis for screening data centers for SSCI during interconnection studies.
