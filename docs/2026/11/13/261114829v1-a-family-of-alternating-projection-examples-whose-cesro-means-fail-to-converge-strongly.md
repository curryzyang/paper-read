## TLDR
This paper introduces a simpler family of counterexamples to strong convergence of the method of alternating projections—and of its Cesàro means and alternating proximal mappings—whose orbits converge weakly but not strongly to the origin while maintaining a positive limiting norm, with explicit lower bounds and algorithmic interpretations.

## Abstract
In 1965, Bregman proved that the sequence of iterates of the method of alternating projections applied to two closed convex sets with nonempty intersection in a Hilbert space converges weakly to a point in the intersection. The question whether this convergence may actually fail to be strong was settled in a seminal paper by Hundal in 2004. He provided a counterexample featuring a halfspace and a closed convex cone generated by a certain curve. While largely elementary, the details of Hundal's construction and analysis are quite involved.
  We revisit this seminal example and present a family of such examples that allows for simpler proofs. Our generating curve combines unit vectors with Gaussian inner products and a height determined by a scalar function. The orbits converge weakly --- but not strongly --- to the origin, while their norms have a positive limit for which we give explicit lower bounds. We also prove that the associated Cesàro means behave likewise; in fact, this is generalized to alternating proximal mappings whose composition has a fixed point. Finally, these orbits allow interpretations in the context of projected-gradient, proximal-point, and gradient-descent algorithms.
