## TLDR
This paper uses the Jacobian Voltage Stiffness Metric to show that both the number and location of grid-forming inverter-based resources significantly affect small-signal system strength, providing a quantitative basis for specifying GFM requirements by resulting strength rather than penetration or unit count alone.

## Abstract
As power systems transition toward inverter-based resource (IBR)-dominated grids, Grid-ForMing (GFM) IBRs are increasingly expected to provide the voltage-source characteristics required to maintain adequate system strength. However, how many GFM IBRs are required, and where they should be located in the grid, remains unclear. This paper investigates the impact of the number and location of GFM IBRs on small-signal system strength. The Jacobian Voltage Stiffness Metric (JVSM), which quantifies the voltage-magnitude and voltage-phase stiffness from frequency-domain Jacobian characteristics, is used as a system-strength measure. The analysis demonstrates that increasing the number of GFM IBRs generally improves system strength; however, configurations with the same number of GFM IBRs can exhibit significantly different bus-wise system-strength characteristics depending on their locations. Thus, this paper provides a quantitative basis for specifying GFM requirements in terms of the resulting system strength, rather than solely in terms of penetration level or the number of GFM IBRs. Case studies on a modified IEEE 39-bus system demonstrate how JVSM can be used to quantify and compare the system-strength impact of different numbers and locations of GFM IBRs, with the resulting strength assessments validated against small-signal oscillatory stability.
